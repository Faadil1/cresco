## Candidate first experimental scenario — not implemented
An adult participant plays a learner with a fictitious $100 weekly budget, $65 essential commitments and an optional $50 purchase. The guardian has preconfigured a minimum reserve requirement. The extra purchase would breach the displayed rule. The participant chooses among Change my plan, Learn why, or Ask for one exception.

The guardian sees the exact proposed fictitious action, remaining commitments and impact; may decline, allow exactly that simulated action once, or deliberately edit the standing practice Key. A changed amount ($49 after approving $50), replay or expired permission must refuse. An approval must never silently widen the next decision. Ask learner to explain their choice and why the rule applied; education is the result of reflection, not a decorative quiz. An unavailable result must display UNKNOWN, not success.

This scenario's Practice policy requires a separate sandbox implementation before any test claims. If mapped to a Solana action, specify a separately evidenced Devnet path. No direct fiat/credit/tokenized securities. Budget and debt/credit/fraud/savings concepts are candidates, not a commitment to build every module.

## Comparative testing, not a claim of market uniqueness
Run consented adult-only usability research, with no actual minors or real financial/identifying data. If existing public demo uses shared households or unverified guardian credentials, **do not** use it for multi-participant data-bearing testing: use an isolated moderated prototype.

Round A (baseline): 6-10 adults view original Family flow and answer independently: Is any money real? Who controls the permanent rule? Does Allow Once change next week's limit? Record exact wrong answers.
Round B (counterbalanced comparison): 6-10 adults evaluate first candidate Decision Practice mock scenario vs existing investment Practice/ordinary financial quiz. Observe correct reasons, decision changes, confusion, time and facilitator assistance. Do not pretend these samples are statistical validation.
Round C (buyer interviews): separate parent buyers, educator/program buyers and institution/fintech buyers. Ask actual prior problem, current workaround and spend, decision maker, trial willingness. A compliment is NOT willingness to pay.
Round D (conditional later): 7-14 day recall/return test of a NEW scenario if eligible consent and secure identity are in place.

| Hypothesis | Decision signal | Disconfirmation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Simulation unmistakable | Every first-round adult understands no real money/stocks | Any person believes paid deposit or actual share was purchased => pause confusing flow |
| One-use rule understood | Heuristic >=8/10 can explain that permanent Key stays unchanged, preregister before interviewing | <7/10 or reliance on facilitator |
| Decision comprehension beats passive education | New scenario produces better unaided trade-off explanation than baseline | Same/worse result, higher friction |
| Relative value to current substitutes | Specific parent or educator cites costly unmet problem and requests pilot | Existing quiz/simulator/parent approval already sufficient |
| No access confusion | Isolated adult testing; no unauthorized cross-role/household actions | Any observed unauthorized action => stop real-account testing |

Sample sizes and thresholds are exploratory heuristics, not statistically powered. Record negative outcomes and losses to follow-up. Do not collect children’s PII in public repos.

## Novelty test against real alternatives
Benchmark Greenlight, Fidelity Youth, Zogo, paper portfolios, ordinary budgeting apps and school materials on the SAME scenario: (a) unaided first-minute understanding, (b) permitted independence inside a visible rule, (c) explainable refusal, (d) exact temporary exception vs permanent widening, (e) reflection, (f) expiry/replay/recovery, and (g) cost, consent and complexity. Documentation silence is not proof of competitor incapacity. Onchain authenticity is an advantage ONLY when actually needed and proven.
